Why is economics underlooked in engineering?

An exploration of how user needs, design decisions and engineering constraints come together to create useful products.

26 October 2026 ·

Introduction

I am studying product design engineering at Loughborough University and currently in my second year. I wouldn’t include this anecdote if I deemed it not necessary however, this is not the case. During my studies the projects we embark on aim to simulate the real world of engineering, but they all forgo a key aspect which is the economic understanding and application to the situation.

Why is it not taught?

I believe the lack of inclusion is due to time constraints, as other areas are deemed more important. I also think that economic understanding and application are not found in the IMechE specification which my degree is accredited by this cannot be verified as the qualification checker is private. Speaking to my peers and they believe that it is not included due to lack of relevancy and not wanting to increase their workload. the latter makes sense, but I fail to see how the former is the case.

What imapct does this have?

Economics is important as it underpins what can be done in reality; much of the work is done in a way to emulate the real world. But disregarding economics and neglecting the issue of finite resources and their costs is foolish as we are not learning how to make concessions when it comes to our designs. Other than budget lack of economic understanding affects how we perceive projects briefs. A prime example of this is our Energy Demand & Engineering Science module. The module aims to teach how to “Evaluate different energy and power sources for consumer products, design options and relevant environmental impact.” As of writing we have vaguely touched on this topic however, the information and data was outdated rendering it erroneous.

Why do I think this is so important?

The main reason why I am so passionate about this topic is due to the everchanging world of economics, recently oil prices have peaked, and the UK will not attempted to rejoin the EU until after a general election. These affect how we should design and market our products. Comprehending basic economic trends will allow for better market demand and viability, I often hear frustration about understanding the market and how much customer willingness to pay for goods. Life cycle analysis and understanding of externalities (unintended cost or benefit experienced by a third party who is not directly involved in the production or consumption of a good or service.) directly relates to engineering; during projects my peers often think about environmental impact as just production and disposal. If they understood externalities it would allow for better design with less carbon emissions during the usage of the product.

Conclusion

By education the up-and-coming engineers about economics it can lead to products that have lower carbon emissions and that are more economically viable. Thus, benefiting us all.
