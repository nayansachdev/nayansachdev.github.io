What makes a good product?

An exploration of how user needs, design decisions and engineering constraints come together to create useful products.

25 October 2026 · Example article

Introduction

A good product does more than perform its intended function. It should solve a genuine problem, be straightforward to use and work reliably for the people it is designed for.

Product design engineering brings together creative thinking and technical problem-solving. It requires designers to consider what users need while also understanding the practical constraints involved in making a product.

Understanding the user

The first step in designing a useful product is understanding the problem. User research can reveal frustrations, existing workarounds and needs that may not be immediately obvious.

These findings can help establish design requirements and provide a way to judge whether a proposed solution is effective.

Balancing design and engineering

A product may look appealing but still fail if it is too expensive to manufacture, difficult to maintain or unreliable in everyday use. Material selection, manufacturing methods, durability and cost all influence the final design.

Good engineering involves balancing these considerations rather than optimising one feature in isolation.

Conclusion

A successful product combines a clear understanding of users with thoughtful design and sound engineering. The most effective solution is not necessarily the most complicated; it is the one that addresses the problem appropriately.
