Product Design Engineering Project

Reducing
Food Waste.

A product design project exploring how a simple smart-home system could help households reduce food waste by making people more aware of food before it goes out of date.

01 — Overview

The challenge

This project focused on the problem of food waste within households, particularly considering the needs of lower-income communities. The aim was to develop a product that could help users keep track of food and encourage them to use it before it becomes waste.

Project focus

Developing a practical solution that could fit into everyday household routines without requiring users to constantly monitor their food themselves.

Design objective

Provide timely reminders about food that is approaching its expiry or is likely to be forgotten.

02 — Problem Definition

Making food easier to remember.

A key issue identified during the project was that food can be forgotten once it has been placed inside a refrigerator. Even when users intend to consume food, it can become overlooked as other items are added.

Food gets forgotten

Items can remain in the fridge until they are no longer suitable to eat.

Users need reminders

A reminder system could bring attention to food at the right point rather than relying entirely on memory.

Affordability matters

The product needed to consider the needs of households where reducing unnecessary food spending is particularly important.

03 — Research & Users

Designing around real users.

The project considered several stakeholders connected to food waste and household food use. This helped broaden the design beyond a single user and encouraged consideration of affordability, accessibility and everyday behaviour.

Households

People who need a simple way to keep track of food stored at home.

Families

Larger households where food usage and organisation can become more difficult to manage.

Local shops

Businesses connected to the wider issue of food distribution and waste.

Foodbanks & volunteers

Organisations involved in supporting people and redistributing food.

Schools & charities

Potential organisations for raising awareness and encouraging better food-management habits.

Local authorities

Wider stakeholders interested in reducing household waste and improving sustainability.

04 — Design Development

From problem to concept.

The design process focused on understanding the problem first, then developing and refining a concept around the needs of the user.

01

Research

Investigated the problem of household food waste and the users affected by it.

02

Problem definition

Identified forgotten food and poor visibility of stored items as important design considerations.

03

Ideation

Explored ways of reminding users about food before it becomes waste.

04

Concept refinement

Developed the strongest idea into a clearer household product concept.

05 — Final Concept

A smarter fridge experience.

The final concept centred around a system that helps the user remember food stored in their refrigerator and alerts them when an item needs attention.

The proposed system

The concept uses reminders to bring food to the user's attention before it becomes waste. Rather than relying on the user to remember every item in the fridge, the system provides repeated prompts so that food is more likely to be used in time.

Awareness

Makes users more aware of food that could otherwise be forgotten.

Reminders

Provides repeated prompts to encourage action before food is wasted.

Everyday use

Designed around a familiar household environment and existing routines.

06 — Product Design Considerations

More than just a reminder.

User needs

The system needs to be simple enough to fit naturally into everyday kitchen routines without becoming an additional burden for the user.

Accessibility

Clear and understandable feedback is important so that users can quickly understand which food needs attention.

Affordability

The design should consider the cost of components and manufacturing so that the solution remains realistic for its intended users.

Future development

The concept could be developed further through improved food identification, better user feedback and more automated monitoring.

07 — Reflection

What I learned.

This project developed my ability to move from a broad social problem towards a more focused product opportunity. It also reinforced the importance of considering users, stakeholders and practical constraints throughout the design process rather than focusing only on the final product.



The project also gave me experience in communicating a product concept through research, design development and evaluation, which is an important part of my development as a Product Design Engineer.
