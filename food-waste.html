Product Design Engineering · Project 02

Reducing
Food Waste.

Exploring how a smart food-storage product could help households keep track of food, improve visibility and reduce the chance of items being forgotten.

01 — The Problem

Food can be easy to forget.

Food stored in a refrigerator can become hidden behind other items or get overlooked during everyday routines. When users lose track of what they have, food may pass its intended use date before it is consumed.

This project explored a product concept intended to make stored food easier to monitor, with particular consideration for households where avoiding unnecessary food waste can make a difference to the food budget.

Refrigerator storage context — food can be spread across shelves and become harder to keep track of.

Visibility

Help users identify and check food without relying entirely on memory.

Reminders

Explore how prompts could encourage users to check food before it is forgotten.

Everyday use

Consider a product that fits into familiar kitchen routines and is straightforward to use.

02 — Concept Development

From sketch to product idea.

The hand-drawn concept helped communicate the proposed form of the product and explore how the user would interact with it. Different views were used to investigate the enclosure, viewing window, controls and internal arrangement.

Initial concept sketch with front, top and other views detailing the proposed design.

Features explored in the sketch

The sketch allowed several design details to be considered before developing the CAD model.

  • Front viewing window
  • LED indicator on the front panel
  • Handle and access to the enclosure
  • Seal and internal divider arrangements
  • Adjustable vent and power connection details
03 — CAD Development

Developing the form.

The concept was translated into CAD to explore the overall proportions, enclosure, front panel and viewing window. Visualising the product in three dimensions made it easier to compare different versions of the design.

CAD development — three design variations with a front viewing window and different-coloured indicator elements.

The models show how the same core concept could be explored through different proportions and visual treatments. The window allows the contents to remain visible, while the indicator provides a clear feature on the front of the enclosure.

04 — Design Process

Turning a problem into a concept.

01 — Understand

Identify the problem

Consider how food is stored and why items may become overlooked in a household refrigerator.

02 — Explore

Sketch possibilities

Use initial sketches to investigate the product's shape, features and arrangement.

03 — Develop

Model in CAD

Translate the concept into three-dimensional models and compare different design variations.

05 — Reflection

Designing for everyday behaviour.

This project explored how a product could address an everyday problem through a combination of physical design and user feedback. Moving from a hand-drawn sketch to CAD helped communicate the concept more clearly and consider how its different features could work together.


Further development could investigate usability, how food information would be entered or detected, how reminders would work, and how the design could be tested with intended users.
