01 — Smart Home Systems

Tonic-Clonic
Seizure Detection

A multi-sensor smart home system designed to identify seizure-like activity through movement and sound, while reducing false positives.

01 — Overview

Designing for safety within the home.

This project explored how smart home technology could be used to improve safety for people who experience tonic-clonic seizures.

Existing solutions including wearable devices, bed sensors and camera systems each provide useful detection methods, but also introduce limitations such as charging requirements, restricted coverage, false positives and privacy concerns.

Our goal was to develop a non-invasive system capable of monitoring the home environment using multiple sensing methods.

02 — The Problem

A seizure can happen anywhere.

Tonic-clonic seizures involve loss of consciousness, followed by body stiffening and rhythmic shaking.

Within the home, this can create risks such as falls in bathrooms, contact with sharp objects in kitchens or difficulties while sleeping.

Research into existing products showed that many solutions were focused on specific environments rather than treating the home as a continuous monitoring environment.

03 — Research & Existing Solutions

No single approach
solved every problem.

01

Wearable devices

Multiple sensors can improve detection, but wearables require regular charging and depend on the user continuously wearing the device.

02

Bed sensors

Useful during sleep, but restricted to the bed and potentially affected by mattress construction and normal sleep movement.

03

Camera systems

Can provide broad monitoring and movement analysis, but introduce significant privacy concerns within personal spaces.

04

Design opportunity

A non-invasive multi-sensor system could combine different signals to improve reliability while avoiding continuous video monitoring.

04 — Concept Development

Four approaches.
One final direction.

01

Infrared camera

Improved privacy compared with conventional video, but remained invasive and could be affected by environmental temperature changes.

02

Audio monitoring

Simple and non-invasive, but vulnerable to false positives from everyday sounds.

03

Pressure detection

Could monitor seated users but would not detect events while standing or walking.

04

Ultrasonic motion

Provided broad room coverage without imaging, but required another sensing method to reduce false positives.

Final concept

The final design combined an ultrasonic sensor with a sound sensor. Both conditions needed to be satisfied before an event was classified, increasing confidence compared with either sensor operating independently.

05 — System Design

Two sensors.
One decision system.

The prototype uses an Arduino-based state system to continuously sample both sensors and evaluate whether the measured pattern meets the criteria for a probable event.

Ultrasonic sensor

Measures changes in distance and converts these into a motion difference.

Sound sensor

Measures sound amplitude and provides a secondary confirmation signal.

Microcontroller

Processes both inputs and determines whether the event criteria are satisfied.

07 — Results

The prototype behaved as intended.

Testing showed that isolated movement or isolated sound did not trigger an alert, while continuous movement combined with sound successfully produced an alert.

The system also demonstrated a fast response once both conditions were satisfied within the same confirmation window.

These results supported the design decision to combine two sensing methods rather than relying on a single input.

08 — Evaluation

Designing for uncertainty.

The prototype does not directly identify a seizure. Instead, it identifies movement and sound patterns that may be consistent with seizure-like activity.

This introduced important limitations. Ultrasonic measurements depend on sensor positioning and the direction of movement, while sound measurements can be affected by background noise.

The system also relies on fixed thresholds, meaning the same settings may not be appropriate for every environment or user.

Future development could investigate adaptive thresholds, wider sensor coverage and more context-aware processing.
